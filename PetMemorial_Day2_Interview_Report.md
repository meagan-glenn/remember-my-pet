# PetMemorial.ai User Research - Day 2 Follow-Up Interview

**Date:** January 30, 2026  
**Time Since Loss:** Approximately 45 hours  
**Participant:** Meagan (33, female, childfree millennial)  
**Pet:** Skylar (dog, 12 years)  
**Context:** Follow-up interview tracking grief progression from acute phase (Day 0) to Day 2

---

## Executive Summary

This follow-up interview documents the emotional and behavioral changes between Day 0 (day of euthanasia) and Day 2, providing critical insights into how grief evolves in the acute phase and what users need from the product at different stages.

**Key Finding:** Grief needs shift rapidly in the first 72 hours. Product features must be accessible non-linearly, allowing users to engage with different creation modes based on their current emotional state rather than forcing a predetermined flow.

**Critical Product Validation:** Video compilation feature is not a "reflection tool" for later—it's an **acute grief survival tool** for Day 2-3 when users need something active to do with their pain.

---

## Emotional State Comparison: Day 0 vs Day 2

### Day 0 (Euthanasia Day)
- **Primary Fear:** "I'll forget her"
- **Emotional Need:** Transformation from pain to joy when remembering
- **Coping Mechanism:** Going through photos, documenting details
- **Physical State:** Acute shock, tears, emotional devastation

### Day 2 (45 Hours Later)
- **Primary Fear:** "I'll never find another dog like her. This was a once in a lifetime relationship. This pain will never go away."
- **Emotional Need:** Sign that she's okay, permission to believe in afterlife/presence
- **Coping Mechanism:** Watching videos on repeat to "be with her however I can"
- **Physical State:** **Somatic grief** - head hurts, stomach hurts, "like I'm in physical pain," randomly crying

**Key Shift:** Fear evolved from memory loss to permanence of pain and irreplaceability of relationship.

---

## Behavioral Patterns (Past 45 Hours)

### Activity Cycles
User describes oscillating between two modes:
1. **Staying busy/distracted:** Building app, going to Costco, washing/folding all of Skylar's blankets, setting up guest room
2. **"Rotting on the couch":** Watching TV (boring shows, started new series), consuming videos/photos constantly

**Quote:** *"I go from shifts of trying to stay busy and distracted to doing nothing but rotting on the couch."*

### Environmental Pain Points
- **Inside the house:** Everything reminds her of Skylar, triggers constant grief
- **Outside the house:** Still thinks of Skylar but fewer constant physical reminders
- **Solution:** Leaving house provides slight relief while maintaining connection

### The Only Thing That Helps
**Quote:** *"The only thing that has been helping me emotionally is looking at her videos and pictures constantly."*

**Shift in media consumption:**
- **First two days (Day 0-1):** Mostly photos
- **Today (Day 2):** Shifted to mostly videos
- **Reason:** *"I want to hear her barks and noises. It's literally all I do all day."*

**Critical Insight:** This isn't memory preservation work. **This is presence work.** She's not trying to remember specific moments—she's trying to BE WITH Skylar the only way she can now.

---

## Grief Triggers & Routine Disruption

### Unexpectedly Hard Moments
**Quote:** *"Going to bed and waking up. Every morning I used to wake up and go straight to her for kisses and rubs and say good morning. Going to bed, every night we used to get good night rubs and kisses and we had a routine for 13 years."*

**Key Finding:** Transitions are the worst. 13 years of muscle memory has nowhere to go. The body reaches for a ritual that can no longer be completed.

---

## Social Sharing & Privacy Needs

### Current Sharing Status
- **Public sharing:** None. Has not posted on social media.
- **Private sharing:** Only told 2 people via DMs
- **Emotional response:** "It made me cry"

**Day 0 vs Day 2:** Privacy needs are **stronger** on Day 2, not weaker. User is less ready to share publicly now than she was immediately after loss.

### Memorial Creation Preference
**Quote:** *"I'd want to do it alone or with my husband."*

No desire for group creation or public collaboration in acute phase.

---

## Spiritual/Existential Needs

### The "Sign" She's Looking For
When asked what sign she wanted that Skylar is okay:

**Quote:** *"I feel like the sign would be a dream. Or medium. Or someone else's experience connecting with me. I'm constantly reading Reddit threads trying to find a story I connect with about afterlife because I'm not religious. It would tell me she's still with me, or frolicking and hunting moles."*

**Key Finding:** User is **building her own afterlife framework** from crowdsourced Reddit stories because she lacks religious structure. She needs:
1. Permission to believe Skylar still exists somewhere
2. Evidence from others' experiences (not doctrine)
3. Specific imagery: "frolicking and hunting moles" (Skylar's personality continues)

**Product Implication:** The memorial could serve as a space where Skylar's presence continues. Not "was" but "is."

---

## Physical vs Digital Memorial Needs

### The "Just Digital" Risk
User expressed concern about Skylar becoming "just a digital memory."

**When asked what she's afraid of:**
- Acknowledged physical memorials (shrines in home) exist but "I don't have her ashes yet"
- Plans to scatter ashes, not keep urn: *"I also think that'd be kind of weird for me"*
- Wants "as many tangible things as I can that aren't urns"

### Solution: Digital + Physical Integration
**User Validation:** Photo book or digital picture frame

**Quote on digital picture frame:** *"Yeah. Someone could also put the frame in a memorial if they wanted."*

**Key Insight:** User recognizes different grief styles. Frame placement = user's choice:
- Integrated into daily life (bookshelf, living room)
- Dedicated memorial space (with collar, candles)

**Product Spec:** Memorial page syncs to digital picture frame
- Rotates through photos/videos
- User can add new content over time
- Frame is "alive" not frozen in time
- Best of both worlds: digital permanence + physical presence

## Video Feature Deep Dive

### AI Video Compilation Feature Validation

**User Preference:** "Version B: You curate, AI assembles"

**Why Version B over Version A (AI auto-picks clips):**
User is already doing curation work—watching videos all day, knowing which bark is "her bark," which moment captures her personality. Doesn't need AI to tell her what matters. Needs AI to handle technical execution.

### Proposed Feature Flow
1. Upload all videos to memorial
2. User scrubs through and clips/marks moments ("this 8 seconds," "this bark")
3. User drags clips into order
4. AI handles:
   - Smooth transitions between clips
   - Audio leveling
   - Rendering into one clean file
   - Suggests tasteful transition styles (cut vs fade)

### Feature Parameters
- **Length:** Up to 10 minutes
- **Music:** Optional, can be added later (not required for initial creation)
- **Captions:** Optional, can be added later
- **Philosophy:** Start simple. Layer complexity when user has emotional bandwidth.

**Quote:** *"Maybe music and captions could be added later. Length could be up to 10 mins."*

---

## CRITICAL FINDING: Video Feature is Acute Grief Tool

### When Would User Engage With This Feature?
**Quote:** *"It feels like a right now thing. To keep me busy."*

**This is not a reflection feature. This is a survival feature.**

User is already watching videos all day. Video compilation gives her something **active to do WITH the grief** instead of just sitting in it.

**Why it matters:** Productive grieving. Creating something, making decisions, curating—but all focused on Skylar. Keeps hands and brain busy while maintaining connection.

**Product Prioritization:** This should be **in MVP**, not post-MVP. For users who cope by staying busy, this is what keeps them engaged with the memorial for hours instead of creating once and abandoning.

---

## Memorial Creation Flow is Non-Linear

### Feature Engagement by Day
When asked what she would do first if she opened PetMemorial.ai:

**Day 0:** Photos first
- Less emotionally demanding
- Muscle memory (already scrolling through them)
- Visual processing easier than writing

**Day 0-1:** Q&A for tribute
- Once shock wears off slightly
- Ready to process through writing

**Day 2:** Videos 100%
- Needs something deeper, more immersive
- Needs to HEAR her, be WITH her
- Needs active project to stay busy

**Product Implication:** Don't force linear flow (tribute → photos → videos). Instead: **"What do you need to do right now?"**

Memorial becomes a **grief companion**—a living workspace users return to over days/weeks, adding layers as they're ready.

**Not a one-and-done creation. An ongoing presence.**

---

## Updated Product Requirements Based on Day 2 Findings

### 1. Non-Linear Creation Flow (CRITICAL)
- Users access photos, tribute, or videos in any order
- Memorial is workspace, not wizard
- Save progress across features
- No forced completion path

### 2. Video Compilation Feature (MVP, Not Post-MVP)
- Upload unlimited videos
- Scrub and clip moments
- Drag to arrange
- AI renders (transitions, audio leveling)
- Export up to 10 min
- Music/captions optional, added later

### 3. Digital Picture Frame Integration (Post-MVP)
- Memorial syncs to physical frame
- Auto-updates when user adds content
- Solves "just digital" fear without forcing shrine aesthetic
- User controls placement/meaning

### 4. Ongoing Memorial Relationship Features
- Anniversary prompts (gentle, not pushy)
- Ability to add memories months/years later
- Return visit incentives (view stats, milestones)
- Memorial as "place where her presence still exists"

### 5. Spiritual/Existential Support
- Not religious framing, but acknowledges need for "signs"
- Community stories feature? (like Reddit threads user is consuming)
- Language that allows for ongoing presence, not just past tense

## Researcher Notes

This interview reveals that **grief is not linear, so the product cannot be linear**. Users need different tools at different moments, and forcing a predetermined flow will fail to meet them where they are.

The shift from Day 0 to Day 2 is stark: privacy needs intensify, fear shifts from forgetting to permanence of pain, and video becomes primary medium because it provides sensory presence.

Most importantly: Video compilation is not a "nice to have later." For users who cope by staying busy, it's the feature that transforms the memorial from a static tribute into an active grief companion.
