# PetMemorial.ai User Research Insights

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Interview Date** | January 28, 2026 |
| **Participant** | Meagan (33, female, childfree millennial) |
| **Context** | Day of euthanasia for 12-year-old dog, Skylar. Acute grief phase. Second pet loss experience (first at age 18). |
| **Persona Match** | Primary persona (Grieving Pet Owner): Strong match. Views pet as family member, emotionally devastated, wants to honor pet's memory. |

---

## Executive Summary

This interview was conducted with a user experiencing acute grief on the day of her dog's euthanasia—the exact moment PetMemorial.ai is designed for. The insights are unusually valuable because they capture real-time emotional needs rather than recalled experiences.

**Key validation:** The core product concept resonates strongly. Price insensitivity is confirmed ($99 feels insignificant after $1,500+ in vet bills). The timing assumption is validated—she would use the product today while already going through photos and videos.

**Key pivot:** The emotional job-to-be-done is different than framed. It's not primarily about validating grief to others ("it wasn't just a dog"). It's about helping the user remember the good years, not just how it ended. The messaging and positioning should reflect this shift.

---

## The North Star Quote

When asked what "success" would look like after using the product, the participant said:

> "Something that tells me I can move on, but not forget. That she's okay. That she was happy in many many moments of life, and that how things ended is not what made up the other 12 years of her life. Something that I can reference back to and see all of those happy memories and experience joy, not hurt and pain."

**Recommendation:** This quote should inform all product messaging, onboarding copy, and success metrics. The goal is transformation from pain to joy when remembering.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The "Disenfranchised Grief" Messaging May Not Resonate

**PRD assumption:** 97% of pet owners have their grief dismissed with "it was just a dog."

**User reality:** "This one no one is questioning if it was 'just a dog.' I think a lot of people understand millennials that are childfree treat them like kids."

**Insight:** The social dismissal problem may be generational. For millennials with pets-as-family norms, the pain point isn't external validation—it's internal processing. The participant experienced dismissal at 18, but not at 33. Consider whether the "validation void" messaging resonates with the primary demographic or if it's solving yesterday's problem.

---

### 2. "Eulogy" Is the Wrong Word

**PRD framing:** "AI Eulogy Assistant" as core feature.

**User reality:** "I actually don't know what a eulogy is."

**Preferred alternatives:** "A love letter? A memory? A tribute?"

**Recommendation:** Rename to "Tribute," "Love Letter," or "Memory" throughout the product. Test these options. "Create a tribute to Skylar" feels warmer and more accessible than "Generate Skylar's eulogy."

---

### 3. Private Creation Before Public Sharing

**PRD assumption:** Users create and share memorials quickly, inviting contributors early in the process.

**User reality:** "I would love to spend the next few days creating something privately, then sharing it with family and telling them they can add their memories too."

**Recommendation:** Design for a deliberate "private creation period" before the memory wall opens. The "invite others" moment should feel like a conscious choice, not an automatic default. Consider: Create → Refine privately → Choose when to share.

---

### 4. Euthanasia Guilt Support Needs to Listen First

**PRD framing:** "Decision Support mode helps with euthanasia guilt by analyzing symptoms/medical context to reframe the decision as 'compassionate timing.'"

**User reality:** "It would help if someone told me you gave her a peaceful ending, but it would also be the same thing my vet said. So I'd need to be able to share my personal feelings and why I am feeling regret."

The user described spiraling "what ifs": "What if I did chemo a week earlier? What if what if what if what if."

**Recommendation:** Generic reframing feels hollow. The AI must first invite the user to express their specific regrets and fears, then respond to THOSE specific concerns. The difference between helpful and hollow is whether it heard her first.

---

### 5. Price Insensitivity Confirmed

**PRD hypothesis:** Pet owners have just paid $300-800 for cremation services, so $49-99 feels insignificant.

**User validation:** "Honestly $99 feels like nothing. Cremation was $300. The euthanasia was $500. The x-rays yesterday were $800. What's $99 to do something nice for me?"

**Recommendation:** Pricing strategy validated. The $99 premium tier may even be underpriced. Consider testing higher price points for premium physical products.

---

### 6. Physical Products: Beyond What's Listed

**PRD offerings:** Memory book, canvas print, QR code plaque.

**User desire:** "I want both [digital and physical]. I'm trying to find as many tangible things as I can to remember her that aren't urns. Like I was looking at jewelry but it's weird. I'd probably get a memory book. I think I'd like to have a blanket or a small stuffed animal that looks like her. Or a unique way to spread her ashes because we've been talking about spreading her ashes in the mountains."

**Recommendation:** Expand physical product roadmap to include:
1. **Comfort items:** blankets, custom stuffed animals resembling the pet (partners like Cuddle Clones)
2. **Ceremony support:** guides or kits for meaningful ash-spreading ceremonies
3. **Non-morbid keepsakes:** items that feel personal rather than funeral-adjacent

---

### 7. The Product Should Be a Place to Return To

**PRD framing:** One-time memorial creation with collaborative wall.

**User desire:** "Maybe someone to talk to about her whenever it comes up. Somewhere to share photos and videos." And later: "Something that I can reference back to and see all of those happy memories and experience joy, not hurt and pain."

**Insight:** The user wants an ongoing relationship with the memorial, not just a one-time creation. Consider features that encourage return visits: anniversary prompts, the ability to add new memories over time, or even an AI companion for ongoing grief processing.

---

## Summary of PRD Recommendations

| Section | Current PRD | Recommended Change |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Problem Statement | Focuses on "validation void" and social dismissal | Add: internal processing needs, transforming painful memories into joyful ones |
| Feature Naming | "AI Eulogy Assistant" | Rename to "Tribute Creator" or "Memory Writer" |
| Memorial Creation Flow | Create and share simultaneously | Add private creation period; make sharing a deliberate later step |
| Decision Support Mode | Analyzes context to provide reframing | Must invite user to share specific regrets FIRST, then respond to those |
| Physical Products | Memory book, canvas, QR plaque | Add: custom stuffed animals, blankets, ash ceremony kits |
| Product Vision | One-time memorial creation | Consider ongoing relationship: a place to return to, add memories over time |

---

## Suggested Next Steps

1. Conduct 3-5 additional user interviews to validate these findings across different demographics and grief stages.
2. A/B test naming options ("Tribute" vs "Love Letter" vs "Memory") with landing page copy.
3. Prototype the "private creation → deliberate sharing" flow and test with users.
4. Revise Decision Support mode prompts to lead with empathetic listening before reframing.
5. Research partnership opportunities for comfort products (Cuddle Clones, custom blanket vendors).
